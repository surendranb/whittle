# Product

## Register

product

## Users

People with thousands of photos in Apple Photos who burst-shoot (5–6 near-identical
frames) and never clean up. Tech-comfortable enough to run a local model
(LM Studio/Ollama) or paste an API key. Primary user today: the developer.
Context of use: a periodic "clean-up session" on a Mac — minutes to an hour,
hundreds of decisions, desire to finish fast and trust the result.

## Product Purpose

Make burst-photo cleanup fast and safe: find groups of similar shots, let a
vision model suggest which to keep and why, and let the human decide.
Success = user clears real gigabytes in one sitting without fearing a wrong
deletion, and the suggestions feel trustworthy enough to accept most of the time.

## Brand Personality

Calm, honest, assistive. Three words: **trustworthy, quick, unobtrusive**.
The model advises, never acts — every deletion passes a human click plus the
macOS confirmation. Tone of copy: plain, factual, no hype.

## Anti-references

- Not a "storage cleaner" scamware app (scare tactics, fake progress, red alarms).
- Not an AI-first toy that hides the photos behind chrome and chat.
- No over-engineering: single window, no onboarding wizard, no accounts.

## Design Principles

1. **The photo is the interface** — controls live on or next to the photos;
   nothing competes with seeing the shots clearly.
2. **Human decides, machine explains** — suggestions always carry a reason;
   nothing is ever pre-marked for deletion.
3. **Never a dead control** — anything in-flight shows progress and can be
   cancelled; every failure says which component failed and what to do.
4. **Show the outcome** — cleared photos and freed space are always visible;
   the payoff is the product.
5. **Works with zero setup** — scanning and manual review need no model;
   AI is an upgrade, not a requirement.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Keyboard-first review flow (arrows + Enter/Delete). Decision states encoded by
icon + color + opacity, never color alone. Target WCAG AA contrast for text
over photos (scrim behind captions). VoiceOver labels on icon-only controls.
