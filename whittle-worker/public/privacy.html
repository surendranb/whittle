Privacy

Short version: your photos stay on your Mac, and the only data we see is anonymous counts.

Your photos

Scanning, grouping and similarity checks run entirely on your Mac. With a local model (LM Studio or Ollama), nothing at all is sent anywhere. With the cloud model (Google AI Studio), only small 768px JPEG copies plus measured numbers — sharpness, face count, blink, tilt — are sent to Google to get a suggestion. Never originals, never names, never locations. Deletion always needs your click plus macOS’s own confirmation, and photos go to Recently Deleted first.

Anonymous usage telemetry

Whittle sends small, anonymous event counts to improve the product. Each install gets a random ID — it is not tied to your name or Apple account. The events are limited to:

  • boot — app opened, model in use
  • scan_completed — photos scanned, groups found, library size (bucketed ranges, not exact counts)
  • suggest_used — backend (local/cloud) and whether a suggestion succeeded or errored
  • suggestion_decided — you accepted the suggestion or overrode it
  • photos_deleted — number of photos and bytes freed

No photo contents, filenames, faces, or your location. At most, the country you connect from (e.g. IN) — your IP address is discarded before anything is stored.

Where it goes

Events go through an open-source relay (whittle-worker) that allows only the events and fields listed above — anything else is dropped at the door — then to PostHog, hosted in the EU. Your IP is never forwarded and PostHog is configured to discard IP data; only the country from the relay is kept.

Your choice

Telemetry is pre-checked on first run and off is a single toggle in the app’s footer, anytime. Turning it off stops new events and clears anything waiting to be sent.
